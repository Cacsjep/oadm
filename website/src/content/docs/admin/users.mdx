Everyone logs in to the server with their own user. Every user has one role, and the role decides what the user may do.
A [device group](../device-groups/) can limit a user to part of the devices.

| Role | What it may do |
|---|---|
| **Administrator** | Everything, also what plugins add later. Cannot be changed. |
| **Operator** | Works with devices and tasks. Changeable; **Reset to default** brings back the defaults. |
| **Viewer** | Sees the devices, live preview and the monitoring pages. Changes nothing. Changeable. |
| Your own roles | What you tick in the matrix. Start empty or as a copy of another role. |

Users, roles, the credential list, server settings, turning plugins on or off and the audit log are for administrators
only. No other role can get them.

## Permissions page

<Image src={users} alt="Permissions page with the users and device groups on top and the roles below" class="screen" />

**New user** adds a user. The buttons of a row edit the user's role and devices, change the password, disable or delete the user. You
cannot change your own role or delete yourself, and the last administrator stays. A new role applies to the user's next
action, without logging in again.

Five wrong passwords in five minutes lock a user for five minutes.

The **Devices** field of a user is **All devices** or a device group. The **Device groups** card below the users
creates and changes the groups: see [Device groups](../device-groups/).

## Roles

<Image src={matrix} alt="The permission matrix: permissions as rows, roles as columns" class="screen" />

Below the users, every permission is a row and every role a column. The permissions are grouped like the client:
**Devices**, **Tasks**, **Event rules**, then one group per plugin. A check box in a group row gives or takes the whole
group. Point at a permission to see what it allows. A warning icon marks permissions that act on the devices with
their stored login, such as removing users on devices or opening the web interface in **Web View**: whoever has them
can change anything on the device.

Live preview and Live View need **Live view**. **Optics Control** adds zoom, focus, PTZ and the RTSP port of a camera. **Change views**
(Live View plugin) creates, changes and deletes the saved views; Viewers open views but do not have it. **Export
recordings** (Edge Recording Export) downloads the recordings of cameras; Viewers only search them.

- **New role** asks for a name and whether to start empty or from another role.
- The menu of a role column renames, copies, resets (Operator, Viewer) or deletes it. A role users have cannot be
  deleted.
- **Save** saves every change at once, after a confirmation that lists what changes per role. **Discard** goes back.

Some tasks have more than one permission, so a role can do the everyday part only: **Upgrade firmware** (upgrade, with
factory default, downgrade), **Users** on devices (add, change, remove), **Applications (ACAP)** (install, downgrade,
start and stop, remove, license keys), **Import configuration** (settings, connection settings) and **Configure devices** (settings,
dangerous parameters). The dialogs show only what the role may do.

A plugin installed later brings its permissions: Operator and Viewer get the plugin's defaults, your own roles get
nothing until you tick it.

## Your role

<Image src={yourRole} alt="Your role window" class="screen" />

Click your name in the title bar, top right, to see your role, what it allows and your devices.

## After the update to roles

Existing administrators become **Administrator**, everyone else **Operator**. The Operator role no longer has the risky
parts: Web View, removing users on devices, firmware downgrades and factory defaults, downgrading and removing
applications, and importing connection settings. Administrators see this once as a notice with **Check roles**; tick
what your operators need.

## Credentials page

<Image src={credentials} alt="Credentials page" class="screen" />

The passwords OADM tries when it adds devices, in the order added, at most 20. Administrators can show or copy a stored
password; every reveal is in the audit log.

## Audit log

<Image src={audit} alt="Audit tab on the Logs page" class="screen" />

**Logs** > **Audit** (administrators) lists who did what: logins, user, role and settings changes, credential reveals,
task runs, removed devices, PKI and server changes. Entries are kept 365 days.
