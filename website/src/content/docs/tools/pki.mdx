The PKI page runs one certificate authority (CA) on the OADM server. OADM uses it to issue certificates for HTTPS and
IEEE 802.1X on your devices. Devices with a certificate from this CA show **Trusted** in the device list without any
change to the operating system.

<Image src={shot} alt="The PKI page with the certificate authorities, the policies and the certificates" class="screen" />

The page has the cards **Certificate authorities** and **Policies** (side by side on a wide window) and **Certificates**.

## Certificate authorities

On the first start OADM creates a CA named "OADM Root CA" plus the server's name, valid for 10 years. Its chips show
how long it is valid, warn when it expires soon and show whether it is in the trusted root store of the server and of
this computer.

The list shows every CA OADM knows: the OADM CA, previous OADM CAs, the Axis device ID roots and the CAs you imported.
**Used by** names the policies that use a CA. **View** shows a CA's certificate.

- **Install in trusted root store** adds the OADM CA to the operating system of the server or of this computer, so
  browsers and other programs trust your devices.
- **Export public certificate** saves the CA certificate as PEM or DER.
- **Back up** saves the CA with its key as a password-protected file (at least 8 characters).
- **Generate new CA** creates a new CA (1 to 30 years).
- **Import CA** > **Own CA with its key** uses your own CA: a PKCS#12 file with its password, or a PEM certificate with
  its key. **Import CA** > **CA certificate only** adds another CA, for example to let its devices onto the network.
- **Remove** (red, with a confirmation) removes an imported or previous CA. A CA that a policy uses cannot be removed.

### Replace the CA

<Steps>

1. Click **Generate new CA** or **Import CA** > **Own CA with its key**.
2. Fill in the dialog. Errors show below the fields.
3. Confirm. OADM tells you how many devices have certificates from the current CA. They keep working until they are
   renewed.

</Steps>

The old CA stays in the list as a previous CA. Devices with its certificates stay trusted. You can export or remove it
there.

## Policies

The policies are the rules for certificates and for the [802.1X server](../radius-server/) and the
[EST server](../est-server/). Filter them with **All**, **Issuing**, **Certificates**, **802.1X**, **EST** and **Revoke**.
**Add policy** adds one; Edit and Delete sit at the end of each row.

| Kind | What it does | Devices |
|---|---|---|
| **HTTPS certificate** | The CA, how many days the certificate is valid (1 to 3650) and the key (ECC or RSA) for **Enable HTTPS** and EST profiles for HTTPS and RTSPS. | no |
| **IEEE 802.1X certificate** | The same, plus the EAPOL version, the EAP identity (MAC address, host name or a custom text) and the CA of your RADIUS server (the OADM CA or one you import), for **Enable IEEE 802.1X** and EST profiles for IEEE 802.1X and MQTT. | no |
| **Server certificate** | The CA, days and key of the certificate of the 802.1X server or of the EST server. One for each. | no |
| **802.1X server** | Minimum TLS version, cipher suites, largest EAP packet and how often cameras sign in again (the switch decides, or every N minutes). One only. | no |
| **Allow CA** | Lets devices with a certificate of this CA onto the network: only devices OADM manages (default) or any device. | no |
| **VLAN** | Puts the devices in a VLAN (1 to 4094). | yes |
| **Block** | Keeps the devices off the network. | yes |
| **Enrollment** | Which EST profiles the devices may use: all, chosen ones, or none. The EST server refuses a camera no Enrollment policy holds. | yes |
| **Sign-in CA** | Cameras may sign in to the EST server with certificates of this CA (the Axis device ID, the OADM CA or a previous OADM CA): for new certificates (renewals too) or for renewals only. | no |
| **Revoke** | Revokes the certificates of the devices, also every new one while the policy exists. | devices and sites |
| **Expiry warning** | How many days before the end a certificate shows as expiring soon. | yes |

At the start OADM has the policies "HTTPS" and "IEEE 802.1X" (365 days, ECC), the server certificates "802.1X server"
(365 days) and "EST server" (90 days), "802.1X server", "OADM CA" and "Axis device ID" (Allow CA, only devices OADM
manages), "All devices" (Expiry warning, 30 days), "All devices" (Enrollment, all profiles) and "Axis device ID" (Sign-in
CA, new certificates) and "OADM CA" (Sign-in CA, renewals). After an update from an older OADM, "HTTPS" and "IEEE 802.1X"
keep the settings of the former Device certificates and IEEE 802.1X cards and RSA keys, and the EST server's **Sign in
with** becomes one Sign-in CA policy per ticked CA.

- Add more certificate policies, for example "HTTPS 2 years"; Enable HTTPS and Enable IEEE 802.1X let you pick one.
- The last HTTPS or IEEE 802.1X certificate policy, one an EST profile uses and the server certificates cannot be
  deleted. A server certificate policy with other days applies to the next certificate; another key makes a new one.

- A device can be in only one policy of each kind: saving names the device that is in two. Each CA is in one Allow CA
  policy and one Sign-in CA policy at most.
- When a later tag or site change puts a device in two policies, both rows show a warning and the safer one applies:
  Block over VLAN, the older of two VLAN policies, the longer of two expiry warnings; of two Enrollment policies a
  camera may use only the profiles both allow.
- A device without a VLAN policy gets no VLAN from OADM: the switch port keeps its own.
- A change applies at once. The days of the expiry warning are also used by the device list's **Certificate expires**
  column, the events **Certificate expires soon** and **Certificate expired** ([Event rules](../event-rules/)) and new
  checks on the [Compliance](../../devices/compliance/) page.
- **Revoke** asks first and says how many certificates it revokes. This cannot be undone.

## Certificates

Every certificate OADM knows: the CAs, the certificates of the 802.1X and EST servers, the ones created, signed or
imported here and the device certificates the OADM CA issued. Each row shows the name or device, use, issued by, valid
until, used by and state. **Search** finds text in every column; filter by state, use and issuing CA.

- **Create** makes a key and a certificate with an issuing policy (name, host names and IP addresses). You save it as a
  .p12 file with a password of at least 8 characters. OADM keeps the key encrypted; it is part of the server backup.
- **Sign request** signs a certificate request (.csr, PEM or DER) of another system. Its key stays there.
- **Import** adds a certificate (.crt, .cer, .pem) or a .pfx / .p12 file with its password. CA certificates belong in
  Certificate authorities.
- Each row: **View**, **Export** (PEM, DER, or with its key as .p12 where OADM holds the key; never a CA key or a
  device's key), **Revoke** and **Delete**. Revoke takes certificates out of use: they no longer get a camera onto the
  network and are no longer renewed. Delete removes certificates nothing uses: revoked or expired ones, and the ones
  created, signed or imported here. A revocation stays in force.

Only administrators with "Manage the certificate authority" and access to all devices can change the list. Users
limited to a device group see the certificates of their devices.

## Use the certificates on devices

Right-click devices on the Devices page and open the **Security** group: Enable HTTPS, Disable HTTPS, Enable IEEE 802.1X,
Disable IEEE 802.1X, Renew certificates, View certificates, Delete certificates, Install certificates and Install CA
certificates. These need AXIS OS 11.11 or later. Each runs as a task with its steps; see
[Tasks](../../devices/tasks/).

## Certificates that renew by themselves

With the [EST server](../est-server/) (OADM PRO) cameras get their certificates from this CA and renew them by
themselves. A camera's renewed HTTPS certificate does not show **Certificate changed**: OADM accepts a new certificate
when this CA (or a previous one) issued it directly for the same camera.

## Good to know

<Aside type="caution">
Keep a backup of the CA. If the server's key is lost, the page shows "CA key cannot be read" and you can only generate
or import a new CA.
</Aside>

- Everything on the PKI page except viewing it needs the permission **Manage the certificate authority**
  (administrators have it). Operators can run the Security tasks on devices.
- Before the CA is installed in the server's trusted root store, OADM shows its fingerprint to confirm. Every PKI action
  is written to the audit log.
- Installing the CA on this computer may ask for administrator rights of the operating system.
