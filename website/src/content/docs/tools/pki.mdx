The PKI page is the one place for certificates in OADM. OADM runs its own certificate authority (CA) and uses it to give
your devices certificates for HTTPS and IEEE 802.1X. Devices with such a certificate show **Trusted** in the device list.

Open **PKI** in the navigation (group Security). The page has four tabs:

- **Certificates**: the CAs and every certificate OADM uses.
- **Policies**: the rules. Which certificate a device gets, which certificates get onto the network, when a certificate
  shows as expiring.
- [**802.1X server**](../radius-server/): OADM as the RADIUS server of your switches (OADM PRO).
- [**EST server**](../est-server/): cameras get and renew their certificates by themselves (OADM PRO).

You see a server's tab when your role may see that server, and the Policies tab when you have access to all devices.

## How it fits together

Two sides work together, and each has its own policies:

| Side | What it is | Set in |
|---|---|---|
| The cameras' certificates | Each camera gets its own certificate for HTTPS or IEEE 802.1X. The **issuing policy** decides which CA signs it, how long it lasts and the key. | Policies: **HTTPS certificate**, **IEEE 802.1X certificate** |
| The servers' certificates | The 802.1X server and the EST server each show one certificate to the cameras, so the cameras know they talk to the right server. | Policies: **802.1X server**, **EST server** |
| Who gets in | The 802.1X server lets a camera onto the network only with a certificate of a CA you trust. The EST server lets a camera sign in only with a certificate of a CA you allow. | Policies: **802.1X trusted CA**, **EST sign-in CA**, **Enrollment**, **VLAN**, **Block** |

A camera with IEEE 802.1X also has to trust the RADIUS server. The **RADIUS server CA** of the IEEE 802.1X certificate
policy is the CA the camera checks the RADIUS server's certificate with. With OADM's 802.1X server this is the CA that
issued the 802.1X server's certificate. With another RADIUS server it is the CA of that server.

## What needs OADM PRO

| Works without a license | Needs OADM PRO |
|---|---|
| The CAs and the Certificates tab | The [802.1X server](../radius-server/) |
| Enable HTTPS, Enable IEEE 802.1X, Renew certificates and the other tasks of the Security menu | The [EST server](../est-server/) and the **Certificate enrollment** task |
| The policies HTTPS certificate, IEEE 802.1X certificate and Expiry warning | The server, 802.1X access and EST enrollment policies |

Without OADM PRO the Policies tab shows only the policies OADM itself uses; the others stay stored and come back with a
license or the 30-day trial. Start the trial on the off page of the 802.1X or EST server, or under
[Settings > License](../../admin/settings/#license).

## Certificates

<Image src={shot} alt="The Certificates tab with the certificate authorities and the certificates" class="screen" />

The tab has two lists, one above the other. Drag the bar between them to give one more room.

### Certificate authorities

On the first start OADM creates a root CA named "OADM Root CA" plus the server's name, valid for 10 years, with an RSA
4096 key. The built-in policies use it. It is a CA like any other: you can replace it in the policies and delete it.

Each row shows the name, **Root CA** or **Intermediate CA**, the issuer, the key and the date it is valid until. A CA
without its key shows **No key** and cannot issue certificates. **Used in** names the policies that use a CA (point at
it to see all). A root CA shows **OS root store: server** and **OS root store: client** once it is in the trusted root
store of the server's computer and of your computer. The list also holds the CAs of the Axis device ID, the certificate
Axis puts into every camera with AXIS Edge Vault.

**Create CA** makes a new CA:

- **Name**: the name devices and browsers show for the CA.
- **Type**: **Root CA (self-signed)** or **Intermediate CA**. An intermediate CA needs **Signed by**: a CA of the list
  with its key. It ends at the latest with that CA.
- **Key**: RSA 4096 (preset), ECC P-256, RSA 2048 or RSA 3072.
- **Valid (years)**: 1 to 30.

A new CA can issue at once: choose it as **Issued by** in a policy. Certificates of an intermediate CA go to the cameras
with the CAs above it. Where a camera refuses that, it gets the certificate alone.

Open a row's actions with **⋯** or a right click:

- **View** shows the certificate.
- **Export** saves the CA certificate as PEM (.crt) or DER (.cer).
- **Install in trusted root store** (root CAs) adds the CA to the operating system of the server and of your computer,
  so browsers and other programs trust your devices. OADM shows the CA's fingerprint to confirm first. On your computer
  the system may ask for administrator rights.
- **Back up** (CAs with their key) saves the CA with its key as a file with a password of at least 8 characters.
- **Delete** (red) takes the CA out of the list. The certificates it issued stay in the list. A CA that a policy uses,
  or that signed another CA in the list, cannot be deleted: change the policy or delete that CA first.

A CA whose stored data cannot be read shows **Cannot be read** and offers only **Delete**.

**Import** adds a CA to the list. It never replaces another CA.

- **CA certificate**: a root or intermediate certificate without its key. Use it to trust devices with its certificates
  on the network (802.1X trusted CA), or as the RADIUS server CA of another RADIUS server.
- **CA with its key**: a .pfx or .p12 file with its password, or a PEM certificate with its key. A CA backup of another
  OADM server works too. The CA can then issue certificates: choose it as **Issued by** in a policy. OADM keeps its key
  encrypted, and the server backup holds it. An intermediate CA keeps the certificates above it from the file.

### Certificates

Every certificate OADM uses now: the servers' certificates, the certificates created, signed or imported here, and the
current certificate of each camera for each use. Each row shows the name, its type as badges (HTTPS, IEEE 802.1X,
Server, Signed, Imported), the issuer, the key and the date it is valid until. **Expires in 12 days** or **Expired** next
to the date says that it needs attention.

- **Group by** sorts the rows into groups by type, by the CA that issued them, or none. Filter by state and by the CA
  that issued them. **Search** finds text in every column, also a serial number.
- **Create**: OADM makes a key and a certificate. Choose the **Use** (HTTPS or IEEE 802.1X), the issuing **Policy**, a
  **Name** and the **Addresses** (host names and IP addresses). You save it as a .p12 file with a password of at least
  8 characters. OADM keeps the key encrypted; the server backup holds it. This is how you make a new certificate for the
  802.1X or EST server.
- **Sign request**: signs the certificate request (.csr, PEM or DER) of another system with an issuing policy. Its key
  stays on that system.
- **Import**: a certificate (.crt, .cer, .pem), or a .pfx or .p12 file with its password.
- Each row's **⋯**: **View**, **Export** (PEM, DER, and **With its key (.p12)** where OADM holds the key; never a CA key or a
  camera's key), then in red **Delete**. With several rows chosen, the menu deletes all of them.

**Delete** removes a certificate from the list. A still valid certificate from a CA of OADM then no longer gets a camera
onto the network through OADM's 802.1X server, and the camera cannot use it to renew ("Certificate deleted in OADM").
The camera gets a new one with Enable IEEE 802.1X, Renew certificates or Certificate enrollment. A certificate that the
802.1X or EST server shows cannot be deleted: choose another one in the server's policy first.

### Current and new certificates

The list shows the certificate each camera uses now, not the history. When a camera gets a new certificate, the old one
stays valid until the camera has the new one installed. Then OADM no longer accepts the old one ("Certificate
replaced by a newer certificate"). A new IEEE 802.1X certificate from the EST server counts as installed once the camera signs in to
OADM's 802.1X server with it; with another RADIUS server the old certificate stays valid.

A camera removed from OADM leaves the list. Its certificates stay valid. Added again, its certificates come back.

## Policies

<Image src={policiesShot} alt="The Policies tab with the policies grouped by kind" class="screen" />

The Policies tab is one table, grouped into **Issuing policies**, **Server policies**, **802.1X access policies**,
**EST enrollment policies** and **Expiry warnings**. Each row shows the name, the type and a summary sentence that says
what the policy does, for example "The 802.1X server puts these devices in VLAN 30." **N policies applied** on the
802.1X or EST server tab opens this tab and highlights that server's policies for a few seconds.

- **Built-in** marks the policies OADM needs. You can change their values but not delete them.
- A red dot before a name says that the policy has a problem, for example a CA that can no longer issue. Point at it to
  read why.
- **Search policies** finds a name, type or summary.
- **Create** opens a panel on the right with every type of policy and what it does. Choose one and click **Next**.
- Click a row to open the policy in the same panel. **Save** stores it and closes the panel. **Cancel** or the close
  button asks first when you changed something (**Discard changes**). **Delete** (red) sits on the left; the built-in
  policies have none.

A server holds up to 200 policies. Changes apply at once. When another user changed the same policy, saving says
"Changed by someone else. Reload."

### The kinds of policies

| Kind | Fields | Devices |
|---|---|---|
| **HTTPS certificate** | **Issued by** (a CA with its key), **Valid (days)** (1 to 3,650), **Key** (ECC P-256 or RSA 2048, made on the camera). Used by Enable HTTPS, Renew certificates and the EST profiles for HTTPS and RTSPS. | no |
| **IEEE 802.1X certificate** | The same, plus **EAPOL version** (1, 2 or 3), **EAP identity** and **RADIUS server CA**. Used by Enable IEEE 802.1X, Renew certificates, Install certificates and the EST profiles for IEEE 802.1X and MQTT. | no |
| **802.1X server** | **Certificate** (one of the Certificates tab with an ECC key), **Minimum TLS version** (1.0 to 1.3), **Largest EAP packet** (500 to 1,400 bytes) and **Reauthentication** (**Switch decides**, or every 10 to 1,440 minutes). One only. | no |
| **EST server** | **Certificate** (one of the Certificates tab). One only. | no |
| **802.1X trusted CA** | **CA**, and whether only devices OADM manages get in (default) or any device with a valid certificate of it. | no |
| **VLAN** | **VLAN ID** (1 to 4094). The 802.1X server puts the devices in this VLAN. | yes |
| **Block** | The 802.1X server refuses the devices, also with a valid certificate. | yes |
| **Enrollment** | The EST profiles the devices may use: all, chosen ones, or none. The EST server refuses a camera no Enrollment policy holds. | yes |
| **EST sign-in CA** | **CA** (the Axis device ID or a CA created in OADM) and **For** new certificates or renewals. Cameras may sign in to the EST server with certificates of this CA. **New certificates** lets them renew too. | no |
| **Expiry warning** | **Days before the end** (1 to 365). The certificates of the devices show **Expires soon** this many days before their end. | yes |

The **EAP identity** is the name a camera gives the switch before its certificate: **MAC address**, **Host name**,
**IP address**, **Last octet of the IP address** or a custom text. OADM's 802.1X server shows it and checks the
certificate, never the name. Another RADIUS server may need a certain form.

**Devices**: **All devices**, or **Choose devices** opens the device picker with sites, tags and single devices. All
devices also holds devices of sites added later; for the 802.1X policies it also holds devices OADM does not manage.

### The built-in policies

A new server starts with these:

| Policy | Kind | Values |
|---|---|---|
| HTTPS | HTTPS certificate | OADM Root CA, 365 days, ECC P-256 |
| IEEE 802.1X | IEEE 802.1X certificate | OADM Root CA, 365 days, ECC P-256, EAPOL version 2, the IP address as EAP identity, RADIUS server CA OADM Root CA |
| 802.1X server | 802.1X server | the shared certificate (see below), TLS 1.2, 1,020 bytes, Switch decides |
| EST server | EST server | the shared certificate |
| All devices | Expiry warning | 30 days |
| All devices | Enrollment | every profile |

And these, which you can delete:

| Policy | Kind | Values |
|---|---|---|
| OADM CA | 802.1X trusted CA | OADM Root CA, only devices OADM manages |
| Axis device ID | 802.1X trusted CA | only devices OADM manages |
| Axis device ID | EST sign-in CA | for new certificates |
| OADM Root CA and the server's name | EST sign-in CA | for renewals |

After an update from OADM 4.1, HTTPS and IEEE 802.1X keep the values of the former cards and an RSA key, and the IEEE
802.1X policy keeps its EAPOL version and EAP identity, so cameras get what they got before. EAPOL version 3 needs AXIS OS
10.1 or later and ECC keys need AXIS OS 12: for older cameras, add a policy with RSA and EAPOL version 1 or 2 (see
[Supported AXIS OS](../../tasks/certificates/#supported-axis-os)). A CA replaced in an older OADM stays in the CA list
as an ordinary CA and gets its own 802.1X trusted CA policy, so its cameras keep getting onto the network.

### When a device is in two policies

- A device can be in only one policy of each kind. Saving names a device that is in two: "10.0.0.48 is in Building B and
  PTZ." Each CA can be in one 802.1X trusted CA policy and one EST sign-in CA policy.
- When a later tag or site change puts a device in two policies, both rows show the red dot and the safer one applies:
  Block over VLAN, the older of two VLAN policies, the longer of two expiry warnings. Of two Enrollment policies a camera
  may use only the profiles both allow.
- A device without a VLAN policy gets no VLAN from OADM: the switch port keeps its own.
- The days of the expiry warning also count for the device list's **Certificate expires** column, the events
  **Certificate expires soon** and **Certificate expired** ([Event rules](../event-rules/)) and new checks on the
  [Compliance](../../devices/compliance/) page.

## Server certificates

<Image src={serverPolicyShot} alt="The 802.1X server policy with its certificate chosen from the Certificates tab" class="screen" />

The 802.1X server and the EST server each show the certificate their policy names. When OADM PRO becomes active (a
license or the trial) or when you turn a server on, OADM makes one certificate for both: **802.1X and EST server**, from
OADM Root CA, with an ECC P-256 key, valid for 10 years. It names the server's computer and its addresses and, while the EST server runs,
the addresses of your site nodes. The policy's **Certificate** field shows the names below it.

This certificate never renews itself. The Expiry warning and the server's status remind you. To use another one:

<Steps>

1. On the Certificates tab click **Create**. Choose **HTTPS** as Use, an HTTPS certificate policy with an ECC key, a
   name and every address the cameras reach the server on (at sites: the site node's address).
2. On the Policies tab open **802.1X server** or **EST server**, choose the new certificate under **Certificate** and
   click **Save**.

</Steps>

The servers use the new certificate at once. Cameras with IEEE 802.1X check the 802.1X server's certificate with their
RADIUS server CA: a certificate from another CA needs that CA on the cameras first (see
[Move to a new CA](#move-to-a-new-ca)).

## Typical tasks

### Turn on HTTPS on cameras

<Steps>

1. Select the cameras on the **Devices** page, for example all of one site. Right-click and open **Security** >
   **Enable HTTPS**.
2. Choose the HTTPS certificate policy and click **Enable**. Each camera gets its own certificate.
3. Optional: in the CA list choose **Install in trusted root store** on the CA of the policy, so browsers on the server and on your
   computer trust the cameras.

</Steps>

### Turn on IEEE 802.1X with OADM's 802.1X server (OADM PRO)

<Steps>

1. Turn on the [802.1X server](../radius-server/) and add your switches with their shared secret.
2. Check the **IEEE 802.1X** policy: **RADIUS server CA** is the CA the hint below it names.
3. While 802.1X is still off on the switch ports, select the cameras, right-click and run **Security** > **Enable IEEE
   802.1X** with that policy.
4. Turn on 802.1X on the switch ports. The **Sessions** list of the 802.1X server shows each camera that signs in.

</Steps>

### Turn on IEEE 802.1X with another RADIUS server

<Steps>

1. On the Certificates tab click **Import** > **CA certificate** and choose the CA certificate of your RADIUS server's
   certificate.
2. Create an IEEE 802.1X certificate policy (or change the built-in one): choose that CA as **RADIUS server CA** and the
   **EAP identity** your RADIUS server expects.
3. Make your RADIUS server trust the CA of the policy's **Issued by**: export it from the CA list (**⋯** > **Export**).
4. Run **Enable IEEE 802.1X** with that policy while 802.1X is off on the switch ports, then turn it on there.

</Steps>

### Let cameras renew by themselves (OADM PRO)

Turn on the [EST server](../est-server/) and run **Security** > **Certificate enrollment** on the cameras. They renew
their certificates halfway through the validity, without a task.

### Renew certificates

Without the EST server, run **Security** > **Renew certificates** on the cameras before their certificates end. The
expiry warning and the event **Certificate expires soon** tell you when.

### Move to a new CA

<Steps>

1. Click **Create CA** and fill in the dialog.
2. With the 802.1X server: add an **802.1X trusted CA** policy for the new CA. Create a certificate for the 802.1X and
   EST server from the new CA (see [Server certificates](#server-certificates)) and choose it in their policies before
   cameras get the new CA.
3. Choose the new CA as **Issued by** in the issuing policies, and as **RADIUS server CA** where it applies.
4. Run **Renew certificates** (or **Enable HTTPS** and **Enable IEEE 802.1X**) on the cameras.
5. Once no camera needs the old CA, change or delete the policies that still name it, then delete it.

</Steps>

The old CA stays in the list until you delete it, so cameras keep working until they are renewed.

## Use the certificates on devices

Right-click devices on the Devices page and open the **Security** group: Enable HTTPS, Disable HTTPS, Enable IEEE 802.1X,
Disable IEEE 802.1X, Renew certificates, View certificates, Delete certificates, Install certificates, Install CA
certificates and, with OADM PRO, Certificate enrollment. They need AXIS OS 6.53 or later. See
[Certificates](../../tasks/certificates/) for each task and the supported AXIS OS versions.

A camera's new HTTPS certificate does not show **Certificate changed**: OADM accepts a new certificate when a CA of the
PKI page that can issue issued it directly for the same camera.

## Good to know

<Aside type="caution">
Keep a backup of your CAs (**⋯** > **Back up** on a CA, or a server backup). If a CA's key is lost, its row shows
**Key cannot be read** and it can no longer issue: create or import another CA.
</Aside>

- Everything on the PKI page except viewing it needs the permission **Manage the certificate authority** and access to
  all devices (administrators have both). Operators can run the Security tasks on devices.
- Users limited to a [device group](../../admin/device-groups/) see the certificates of their devices only, and no
  Policies tab.
- Every change is written to the audit log.
