The PKI page runs one certificate authority (CA) on the OADM server. OADM uses it to issue certificates for HTTPS and
IEEE 802.1X on your devices. Devices with a certificate from this CA show **Trusted** in the device list without any
change to the operating system.

<Image src={shot} alt="The PKI page with its tabs, the policies and the certificates" class="screen" />

The page has four tabs: **Certificates** (every certificate, the CAs among them), **Policies**,
[**802.1X server**](../radius-server/) and [**EST server**](../est-server/). You see a server's tab when your role may see
that server.

## Certificate authorities

On the first start OADM creates a CA named "OADM Root CA" plus the server's name, valid for 10 years. The CAs are rows
of the certificate list: the OADM CA, previous OADM CAs, the Axis device ID roots and the CAs you imported. A CA imported
with its key shows **Can issue**. **Used in** names the policies that use a CA (point at it to see all of them). The OADM
CA's row shows **OS root store: server** and **OS root store: client** once it is in the trusted root store of the
server's computer and of this computer. Open a row's actions with **⋯** (or a right click):

- **Install in trusted root store** (OADM CA) adds the OADM CA to the operating system of the server and of this
  computer, so browsers and other programs trust your devices.
- **Export** saves the CA certificate as PEM or DER.
- **Back up** (OADM CA) saves the CA with its key as a password-protected file (at least 8 characters).
- **Remove** (red, with a confirmation) removes an imported or previous CA. A CA that a policy uses cannot be removed.

**Import** in the toolbar of the CAs adds a CA to the list; it never replaces the OADM CA:

- **CA certificate**: a root or intermediate certificate without its key, for example to let its devices onto the
  network.
- **CA with its key**: a PKCS#12 file with its password, or a PEM certificate with its key. It can then issue
  certificates: pick it as **Issued by** in a policy. OADM keeps the key encrypted like the OADM CA's key; it is part of
  the server backup. An intermediate CA keeps the certificates above it from the file, and they go with its certificates.

### Replace the OADM CA

<Steps>

1. Click **New OADM CA** (1 to 30 years).
2. Fill in the dialog. Errors show below the fields.
3. Confirm. OADM tells you how many devices have certificates from the current CA. They keep working until they are
   renewed.

</Steps>

The old CA stays in the list as a previous CA. Devices with its certificates stay trusted. You can export or remove it
there.

## Policies

The policies are the rules for certificates and for the [802.1X server](../radius-server/) and the
[EST server](../est-server/). The **Policies** tab shows them in cards: **Check a device**, **Certificates for cameras**,
**802.1X network access** and **EST**.

- The policies OADM needs are settings on their card: **HTTPS**, **IEEE 802.1X** and **Expiry warning**, the
  **802.1X server** and the **EST server**. Change a value and click the **Save** below it; **Not saved** shows until you
  do. Errors show below the fields.
- Your own policies are short lists on the same card: **Other issuing**, **Expiry warnings for some devices**,
  **Trusted CAs**, **VLANs**, **Blocked**, **Enrollment** and **Sign-in CAs**. **Add** adds one to that list (Other
  issuing asks HTTPS or IEEE 802.1X); Edit and Delete sit in each row's **⋯**. The Enrollment policy "All devices" is the
  first row of its list and shows **System**: it cannot be deleted.

### Check a device

Click **Choose a device** and pick a device. OADM lists what applies to it, each line with the policy that decides it:
the certificates Enable HTTPS and Enable IEEE 802.1X issue, when its certificates show the expiry warning, whether the
802.1X server lets it in (with which CAs), blocks it or puts it in a VLAN, and which EST profiles it may use.

| Kind | What it does | Devices |
|---|---|---|
| **HTTPS certificate** | **Issued by** (the OADM CA or a CA imported with its key), how many days the certificate is valid (1 to 3650) and the key (ECC or RSA) for **Enable HTTPS** and EST profiles for HTTPS and RTSPS. | no |
| **IEEE 802.1X certificate** | The same, plus the EAPOL version, the EAP identity (MAC address, host name or a custom text) and the CA cameras check the 802.1X server with (**OADM 802.1X server**: the CA of the 802.1X server policy, or one you import), for **Enable IEEE 802.1X** and EST profiles for IEEE 802.1X and MQTT. | no |
| **802.1X server** | The server's certificate (Issued by, days, ECC key), minimum TLS version, cipher suites, largest EAP packet and how often cameras sign in again (the switch decides, or every N minutes). One only. | no |
| **802.1X trusted CA** | Lets devices with a certificate of this CA onto the network: only devices OADM manages (default) or any device. | no |
| **VLAN** | Puts the devices in a VLAN (1 to 4094). | yes |
| **Block** | Keeps the devices off the network. With **Also revoke their IEEE 802.1X certificates** it revokes them too, also every new one while the policy exists (then devices and sites only). | yes |
| **EST server** | The EST server's certificate: Issued by, days and key. One only. | no |
| **Enrollment** | Which EST profiles the devices may use: all, chosen ones, or none. The EST server refuses a camera no Enrollment policy holds. | yes |
| **EST sign-in CA** | Cameras may sign in to the EST server with certificates of this CA (the Axis device ID, the OADM CA or a previous OADM CA): for new certificates (renewals too) or for renewals only. | no |
| **Expiry warning** | How many days before the end a certificate shows as expiring soon. | yes |

At the start OADM has the policies "HTTPS" and "IEEE 802.1X" (365 days, ECC), "802.1X server" (its certificate 365
days), "OADM CA" and "Axis device ID" (802.1X trusted CA, only devices OADM manages), "All devices" (Expiry warning, 30
days), "EST server" (90 days), "All devices" (Enrollment, all profiles), "Axis device ID" (EST sign-in CA, new
certificates) and "OADM CA" (EST sign-in CA, renewals). After an update from an older OADM, "HTTPS" and "IEEE 802.1X"
keep the settings of the former Device certificates and IEEE 802.1X cards and RSA keys, and the EST server's **Sign in
with** becomes one EST sign-in CA policy per ticked CA.

- Add more certificate policies, for example "HTTPS 2 years"; Enable HTTPS and Enable IEEE 802.1X let you pick one.
- The policies OADM needs and a certificate policy an EST profile uses cannot be deleted. Another number of days applies
  to the next server certificate; another key or CA makes a new one.
- A CA a policy names cannot be removed: change the policy first.

- A device can be in only one policy of each kind: saving names the device that is in two. Each CA is in one 802.1X
  trusted CA policy and one EST sign-in CA policy at most.
- When a later tag or site change puts a device in two policies, both rows show a warning and the safer one applies:
  Block over VLAN, the older of two VLAN policies, the longer of two expiry warnings; of two Enrollment policies a
  camera may use only the profiles both allow.
- A device without a VLAN policy gets no VLAN from OADM: the switch port keeps its own.
- A change applies at once. The days of the expiry warning are also used by the device list's **Certificate expires**
  column, the events **Certificate expires soon** and **Certificate expired** ([Event rules](../event-rules/)) and new
  checks on the [Compliance](../../devices/compliance/) page.
- A Block policy that also revokes asks first and says how many certificates it revokes. This cannot be undone.

## Certificates

Every certificate OADM uses now: the CAs, the certificates of the 802.1X and EST servers, the ones created, signed or
imported here and each camera's current certificate for each use. Once a camera has its new certificate installed, OADM
revokes the one it replaced. A camera removed from OADM leaves the list; added again, its certificates come back. Each row shows the name (with the key and the issuer
below), its type as badges (for example Root CA, OADM CA, HTTPS, Imported), valid until (with Expires in 12 days, Expired
or Revoked when it needs attention) and what uses it. **Group by** groups the rows by type, by the CA that issued them,
or not at all. **Search** finds text in every column; filter by state, use and issuing CA.

- **Create** > **Certificate** makes a key and a certificate with an issuing policy (name, host names and IP addresses).
  You save it as a .p12 file with a password of at least 8 characters. OADM keeps the key encrypted; it is part of the
  server backup.
- **Sign request** signs a certificate request (.csr, PEM or DER) of another system. Its key stays there.
- **Import** > **Certificate** adds a certificate (.crt, .cer, .pem) or a .pfx / .p12 file with its password.
- Each row's **⋯**: **View**, **Export** (PEM, DER, or with its key as .p12 where OADM holds the key; never a CA key or a
  device's key), then in red **Revoke** and **Delete**. Revoke takes certificates out of use: they no longer get a camera
  onto the network and are no longer renewed. A revoked camera certificate leaves the list until the camera gets a new
  one. Delete removes certificates nothing uses: expired ones, and the ones created, signed or imported here. A revocation stays in force. With several rows chosen, the menu revokes or
  deletes all of them.

Only administrators with "Manage the certificate authority" and access to all devices can change the list. Users
limited to a device group see the certificates of their devices.

## Use the certificates on devices

Right-click devices on the Devices page and open the **Security** group: Enable HTTPS, Disable HTTPS, Enable IEEE 802.1X,
Disable IEEE 802.1X, Renew certificates, View certificates, Delete certificates, Install certificates and Install CA
certificates. These need AXIS OS 6.53 or later; before AXIS OS 12 choose policies with an RSA key (the supported
versions: [Certificates](../../tasks/certificates/#supported-axis-os)). Each runs as a task with its steps; see
[Tasks](../../devices/tasks/).

## Certificates that renew by themselves

With the [EST server](../est-server/) (OADM PRO) cameras get their certificates from this CA and renew them by
themselves. A camera's renewed HTTPS certificate does not show **Certificate changed**: OADM accepts a new certificate
when a CA of the PKI page that can issue (or a previous OADM CA) issued it directly for the same camera.

## Good to know

<Aside type="caution">
Keep a backup of the CA. If the server's key is lost, the page shows "CA key cannot be read" and you can only generate
or import a new CA.
</Aside>

- Everything on the PKI page except viewing it needs the permission **Manage the certificate authority**
  (administrators have it). Operators can run the Security tasks on devices.
- Before the CA is installed in the server's trusted root store, OADM shows its fingerprint to confirm. Every PKI action
  is written to the audit log.
- Installing the CA on this computer may ask for administrator rights of the operating system.
